# Alignment Interview Guide

## Technique

- **Batch, don't interrogate.** Ask 2–4 related questions per round, in plain language. Twelve questions at once gets twelve shallow answers; one at a time feels like a deposition.
- **Reflect back every round.** "So what I'm hearing is..." in your own words, not theirs. The gaps between their words and your paraphrase are where misalignment lives.
- **Chase the concrete.** When you get an abstract answer ("it should feel simple"), ask for an example ("simple like which app you already use?"). Concrete referents transfer; adjectives don't.
- **Notice what they don't say.** No mention of money? Ask about the business model anyway. No mention of who else is involved? Confirm they're solo. Unasked questions become later surprises.
- **Push back in the interview, not in the brief.** If scope, timeline, and resources don't reconcile, or two goals conflict, raise it as a question. The brief should record resolutions, not bury tensions.
- **Adapt depth to the project.** A personal-use utility may need one round; a commercial consumer app may need three or four. Stop when new answers stop changing the brief.

## Question bank

Pick what's relevant; skip what the user already volunteered. These are themes to cover, not a script.

### The idea
- What does the app do, in one sentence? What problem does it solve, and for whom?
- What triggered this idea — a personal frustration, a gap you spotted, a request from someone?
- What's the one thing this app must do brilliantly, even if everything else is mediocre?
- What existing app is this most like, and what's wrong with that app?

### Audience (hunches only — Stage 1 verifies)
- Who do you picture using this? Describe one real or imagined person.
- How do they solve this problem today?
- Are the buyer and the user the same person?

### Platform & technical constraints
- Any platform already decided (iOS/Android/web/desktop/cross-platform)? Decided, or just assumed? What's driving it — audience, your skills, device features needed?
- Which existing skills/stack do you want to leverage? Anything you refuse to learn for this?
- Does it need anything platform-gated: camera, sensors, offline, push, widgets, background processing, App Store distribution?

### Business & success
- Is this for income, portfolio, personal use, or a step toward something bigger?
- If income: paid, freemium, subscription, ads, or undecided? What's the pricing instinct?
- One year after launch, what result makes you call this a success? What result makes you kill it?
- What's the smallest version you'd still be proud to ship?

### Resources & time-frame
- Who's working on this and how many hours a week, realistically?
- Any external deadline, or is it "done when it's done"?
- Budget for anything paid — assets, accounts, services, testing participants?

### Taste & non-goals
- Apps whose look/feel you admire? Any you hate?
- Any strong opinions already fixed: name, colors, tone, features that must NOT exist?
- What's explicitly out of scope for v1, even if users ask for it?

### For existing/relaunch projects
- What exists already, and what's worth keeping?
- What does current usage/feedback say (even anecdotally)?

## Red flags to surface before certifying

- **Scope–resource mismatch**: the described feature set cannot be built in the stated time at the stated hours.
- **Audience of everyone**: "it's for anyone who..." usually means the audience is undefined. Force a first-user choice; Stage 1 can broaden it with evidence.
- **Success undefined or unmeasurable**: "people like it" cannot gate anything. Get at least one number or observable event.
- **Solution in search of a problem**: the user loves a mechanism, not an outcome. Not fatal, but Stage 1 research becomes load-bearing.
- **Conflicting goals left standing**: e.g. "dead simple" + "power-user feature parity". Record which wins.
